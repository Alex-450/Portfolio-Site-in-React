To make a loaf, start by refreshing the starter.

## To refresh the starter

---

Put 100gm of the starter into a bowl and add:

- 100gm white bread flour.
- 50ml water

1. Mix to a stiff dough.
2. Put the resulting dough into a clean jar.
3. Leave the lid off the jar for about a day – until the dough has risen to 2-3
   times its original size.

This is your refreshed starter. Put on the lid and it will keep in the fridge
for up to two weeks.

## Sourdough loaf

---

Take c. 150gm of the starter (whatever's left after you've refreshed the
starter), and add:

- 500gm any bread flour
- 2 tsp salt
- 300 – 350ml warm water (the warmth isn't essential but gets the dough rising)
- squirt of honey (optional)

1. Mix the ingredients to a pliant dough in a mixer/by hand. Don't knead.
2. Cover for 30 mins.
3. Fold and stretch the dough, but don't knead. Wet your hands to avoid them
   sticking to the dough.
4. Leave to rise in a warm place.
5. Repeat the fold and stretch and leave to rise a second time.
6. Repeat the fold and stretch for a third time and shape for baking.
7. Once the dough has risen for the third time, set the oven at 240-250C and
   place a tin of hot water at the bottom of the oven.
8. Bake in a Dutch oven (a large cast iron casserole pot) with the lid on for 20
   minutes, then remove the lid and bake for a further 25 minutes.

Or (the way I do it):

Do the final rise in a bread proofing basket (banneton) and bake on a baking
stone for 15 minutes at 240C, and 30 minutes on 200C.

NB If the three lots of rising take too long, and it's getting late, put the
dough in the fridge and carry the process on in the morning, just give the dough
time to warm up again first.
